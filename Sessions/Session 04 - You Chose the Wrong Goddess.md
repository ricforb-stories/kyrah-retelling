# Session 4 — July 3, 2026 — "You Chose the Wrong Goddess"

**Level:** 1 → 2
**Party Members in Attendance:** [[The Party/Ellion|Ellion]], [[The Party/Darius|Darius]], [[The Party/Xhazag (Kaz)|Xhazag (Kaz)]], [[The Party/Athalea|Athalea]]

## Our Adventure

### The Cave

It was still raining hard, Kaz still carrying the ruined tent over [[NPCs/Kyrah|Kyrah]] as an umbrella. From the top of the hill they could see miles of mountains to the west and fog lying thick over the low country, and they picked up the boar's trail: split tree trunks and splatters of blood, ending at the mouth of a cave. From inside came ear-splitting grunts and squeals.

The entrance was enormous — twelve meters tall, wide enough for four of Kaz side by side. Nobody knew how to kill an unkillable boar. [[The Party/Athalea|Athalea]] suggested the diplomatic approach first; Kyrah climbed the nearest tree; Kaz stood behind another, which made it look like a tree with two horns sticking out of it. They had one resurrection scroll left between them, and the -4 from their last one was only down to -3.

Then the boar charged out of the cave, gored one of the trees in front of them and flung the trunk aside like a rag doll, and drew itself up to its full height: an ancient boar with fierce red eyes and bloodied yellow tusks, some ten spears bristling from its hide. Most of the party were caught by surprise.

### The Fight

Athalea opened by dropping a five-foot block of ice, frozen from the rainwater she'd gathered with *shape water*, straight onto it. It did nothing. The boar charged Darius and Ellion, and Darius dodged its tusks.

Darius ran up and pulled out one of the spears — and the wound closed instantly. There was no blood around any of the holes.

Then the boar turned its mind on them. A radiant energy seemed to seize everyone but Athalea, twisting their thoughts and making their actions wild and uncontrolled; one after another they failed to shake it. Ellion charged his little club with the energy of nature and smacked it for nine magical damage. Kaz, enraged, breathing smoke from his nostrils like a bull, swung at nothing.

Athalea, in Celestial, asked it to stop — it could speak, so it might be reasoned with, and she apologized for having thought it was a pig. She and Darius were the only ones who could understand it; to the others it was just squealing. What it said, when Darius struck it with *toll the dead*, was aimed straight at him — his own spell turned back on him, and a voice in his head: ***"You chose the wrong goddess today."***

Kaz went for a different approach and grappled it (22 against its 20, twice). He got it from behind in a full nelson, like holding a pig — soft, warm, squirming, and trying to turn its head to gore him — and held it there, shouting at it the whole time: *Let us help you! How do we lift your curse?* *"Lift yours first, you mudblood."* He dislodged another spear with his free hand on a natural 20. That wound closed too.

It hurt them badly. Ellion went down under twelve damage with eleven hit points to spare it. Athalea kept everyone upright between insults — *"It's an obvious thing you never had a mother, because a mother wouldn't birth something that ugly"*; *"Stop hurting my friends. It won't make you prettier"* — and whispered Ellion awake with *healing word*: *"It's not the time for a nap. You had one a couple of hours ago."* Ellion lit a bonfire under the boar while Kaz lifted it high enough that the flames only reached the boar.

Darius prays to two goddesses, Lutheria and Mytros. Partway through the fight, healing Kaz, he heard one of them ask him: *"Why would you betray me like that?"* Kaz, still holding the boar with one arm, refused to let Darius spend himself on him: *"Don't give me your life force. I don't want my life prolonged if it means the end of yours."*

Darius had a closer look at its belly — pinkish, eight spears still in it, no marks — and thought hard about the curse. Every curse is the consequence of something; every curse can be lifted; and he had no idea how, for this one or for his own.

The boar shook off whatever the party threw at its mind three times, and gored Kaz twice until he dropped — ribs showing, *"but my horns are still bigger, for the record"* — and then charged him on the ground for five dice of damage. Ellion healed him and fed him goodberries.

Ellion's answer to the whole thing, in the end, was rocks. He picked up three pebbles, charged them with nature's energy into *magic stones*, left two glowing green in the grass beside Kaz, and threw the third. His last stone took the boar right between the eyes, and it fell to its knees.

As it fell, each of them heard it in their heads:

> *"Know this, mortals: your sins cannot be atoned. An ancient power sleeps in the heart of Thylea. And when it awakens, the age of heroes will come to an end. Just as my broken body falls to ruin and decay, so too will all mortal things come to death and darkness. No prophecy will save you."*

Kyrah jumped down from her tree. *"I told you you wouldn't fail."*

Someone poked the body with a knife. Now it went in; what came out was barely blood at all, almost see-through, like Ellion's sap. When they went to collect some in a waterskin, Kyrah stopped them. That was a bad idea.

### Whose Sacrifice?

Then came the most important business of the day: whom to give it to.

- **Kaz** wanted his father, [[NPCs/Pythor|Pythor]], and would not be able to look him in the eye otherwise. He'd also accept Mytros, or Kyrah as goddess of music.
- **Athalea** argued for **Lutheria** — the lady of death itself, who might then let them out of her grasp now and then, since they'd already died once and would likely die again. Return the creature to its maker.
- **Ellion** wanted **Thylea** — the land itself, harvest and the seasons; no one could be angry at a sacrifice to her.
- **Darius** would give it to Sydon, and only Sydon — appease the Titan who had just killed them. *"No,"* said Kaz. *"You cannot be sincere enough."*

It turned into a long argument about the Titans. Was Thylea dead, or alive, or decaying beautifully? *"Have you ever seen a flower blossom on a corpse?" "The proof of her life is walking right next to you."* What about her husband, Kentimane, the protector with too many heads, who guards her from outside? What do the five gods think of the Titans after five hundred years? Should they give power to the side that had just tried to kill them? Should they balance the scales? *"I do find balance a good thing. But I also find being on the winning team a better thing."* Kaz reminded them he'd arrived in the city with nothing but the hooves on his feet and the horns on his head, and knows something about surviving.

When they listed every deity each of them would accept, two names had the widest support — Thylea and Kentimane — so they flipped a coin between them. It came up **Thylea**.

Ellion led the rite. He built a bonfire under the carcass and handed each of them a branch of dried mistletoe, and as it burned he spoke: *"Thylea, mother of earths and roots and breath, accept a portion of what has been taken. May it return to the earth."* When the fire was almost done they each threw their mistletoe in, and each branch went up in a small green puff of magic.

Everything went still. The fire burned down quickly. The crackle of the mistletoe gave way to a great song of birds in the trees, absent while the boar lived, and the woodland creatures stepped out of their hiding places — stags, squirrels, birds, rabbits, a beaver — and stood in a circle around the fire, staring at the party for a whole minute, as if waiting. Then they nodded, and went back into the woods. The rain stopped.

### Mr. Flufster

Through all this [[NPCs/Mr. Flufster (Master Flamster)|Mr. Flufster]] had been in Athalea's pocket, warm and dark and unguided. Now he broke and ran. Ellion caught him and held on while he screamed and bit, and it took several tries to calm him (Animal Handling) — *no one will hurt you, no one will eat you, you are safe.* The party wondered aloud whether they were taking a little creature away from its forest. Ellion had promised him.

With *guidance* back, Mr. Flufster was himself again, and his price had gone up: not enough blood to cover him, but **a basin, filled to the brim.** Kaz, out of character, offered the bird-skull flask of blood; Ellion checked it with *detect magic* — just a bottle, just a skull, just blood — and decided it would feel wrong to kill a bird right after a sacrifice like that. He would wait for a better moment. When the *guidance* faded again, Mr. Flufster sounded lost, like a stuck recording, and the only word left in him — and in all the birds around them — was *fire.*

### The Dead

The cave was only fifty feet deep, and it stank: stagnant water, gore and boar dung. Athalea went in, stepping through the blood and avoiding the dung, and found two fresh corpses, still warm and shredded, among a great deal of older gore. Between them they carried 25 gold and 37 silver, and one had a spear of pale metal unlike anything the party had seen.

It is customary to bury or burn the dead, and not to rob them. Athalea insisted on at least putting coins on their eyes, and they were to be gold — it turned out Kaz's uncle had told him the fare for the ferryman was copper; it wasn't. Only three of the bodies still had heads.

Darius didn't see the point. These weren't warriors, they were villagers; they weren't his people. Put them in the fire and be done. *"It's just a body."* Kaz and Athalea pushed back hard — the dead deserve respect whoever they were; without the rites, souls stay where they fell and rot there, and that's where the malformed, creeping things come from. Were you raised under a rock?

They dug for five hours: four separate graves, and a shared one for everything that couldn't be told apart. Then, with the last of the liquor from Kaz's flask, Athalea performed the rite in silence — a strange waltz between the graves, accompanied by bells that everyone could hear and no one could see — and ended it with: *"May the afterlife be kinder to you."* Ellion sifted the bonfire and found only bones and ash.

The Oracle's temple, Kyrah said, was about a day's travel on from the tavern; she lives in seclusion. They set off for the tavern.

### The Secret

On the road, the question of where everyone would sleep came up. Athalea would not share a tent with Darius again, or a room, not even in four separate corners. Kaz pointed at the marks on his neck — a lot of small dots — and asked for a conversation about consent: a sleeping person cannot give it. Nobody bit anybody, said Darius. Something did, said Kaz. Was Darius's saliva poisonous?

Darius finally tried to get ahead of it. He asked the party to swear an **oath of peace** — no harm to him, ever — so that his secret would be safe. The answer was no. *"Honey, there is no secret in the world you can share with me to which I will respond with an oath of peace toward the person who literally got me killed by a Titan."* They'd known him for two days. Athalea added that she would put every one of his secrets in the final epic poem, because that's what bards are for — the history of Thylea was being written right now, and she would not conceal any of it.

So he told them. He is a medusa. The snakes on his head are real; they hiss; they bit Kaz. Ellion and Athalea already knew — Ellion had pulled the helmet off and stood there blinking, and Athalea had seen — and had decided to keep it quiet. Kaz was the only one who hadn't known. He had heard rumors that the snakes were only "down there," which is why he'd slept with four layers on.

The curse, Darius said, came from an ancestor generations back — an elf who came to Thylea from outside and did something to the goddess Mytros; he wasn't sure exactly what, and the party assumed the worst. It has run in his family since. He, his brother and his sister all carry it. It shows itself at sixteen. He has never met anyone with it older than twenty-five, because they are hunted. There is no record in all Thylea's history of anyone breaking it, only legends. His order was founded to give people like him a chance: to protect them, and to find a way.

That was when Athalea and Kyrah lost their tempers, both at once. The problem wasn't the snakes. A curse, in this land, is what the world does to someone who has done something so vile it revolts against them — a wound on the world. And his family had gone on having children, generation after generation, knowing they'd pass it on, without repenting and without breaking it. *"Cursed blood should not reproduce,"* said Kyrah. Darius kept saying he is who he is, and that the only thing he dislikes is how people react to him, and then that he wants to find a way out; they told him he'd given ten different answers and they couldn't tell which one was true. Did he want to change, or did he just want people to stop looking at him?

Kyrah settled it. She holds his oath of servitude, and she used it: **he is never to have children, unless he breaks the curse.** She sat down under a tree and said she would not lead any of them to the Oracle until he answered. He swore it: *"I pledge an oath that I will not have children until I find out how to get rid of this curse."*

Then the rest came out. Asked how he could be better, the answer was simple: start by treating the dead with respect, and the living after that. Why had the hunters deserved less of a rite than his own dead? The rites matter to the families and the souls, whether or not he believes in them. *"Maybe you're right. I'm sorry."* Sorry is good, they told him. Now do better.

Kaz, calmly, suggested that instead of asking for oaths from people he'd hurt, he offer one — an oath of protection to them, freely, not as a trade. *"I'm not asking you anything. I'm making a suggestion."* He didn't make it.

Kyrah took his face in both hands and slapped his cheeks. He had a chance to become a hero. But if he lied to her one more time — not about what he'd done, but about what was in his heart — she would think of something. She told him what she'd thought of the party so far: very different, a bit weird, quirky, but cool; and one of them who'd asked, at their first meeting, what the payment would be, and then called a god's followers bastards in the middle of a sacrifice. *"I'm not saying it's all bad. I'm saying some of your choices are questionable."* Darius explained himself at last: someone in his family did something terrible, and he doesn't want to spend every day hating himself for it, and he doesn't want to live without hope. So he's looking for the way.

Athalea pointed out that he wasn't alone: her own people carry a curse too, and have learned to live with it. Ellion said dryads can't be cursed — dryad privilege — and that his people can simply go back to their birth tree and sleep, if they want to. The questions turned, inevitably, to the snakes. Can he sleep without the helmet? Yes. Do they sleep when he does? No. Can they wake him if something comes? He's never tried. Do they have bad hair days, lay eggs, eat mice? He doesn't know. Do they petrify people if they feel threatened? Just try not to harm them.

They agreed to keep watch in turns from now on. By the fire, with Mr. Flufster still murmuring *fire* in the background, everyone reached **2nd level**.
