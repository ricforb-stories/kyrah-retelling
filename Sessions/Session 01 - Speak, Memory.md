# Session 1 — May 29, 2026 — "Speak, Memory"

**Level:** 1
**Party Members in Attendance:** [[The Party/Ellion|Ellion]], [[The Party/Darius|Darius]], [[The Party/Xhazag (Kaz)|Xhazag (Kaz)]], [[The Party/Athalea|Athalea]]

## Opening Invocation

> Speak, memory. Speak, and tell a tale of the saviors — oh heroes, oh champions — speak of the deeds of the Dragonlords, and tell us their tale once again.
>
> Speak of one born of rural root and ancient silence, who walked between the world of settlers and the world of the fey, who tended to the old ways when all others had forgotten them. *([[The Party/Ellion|Ellion]])*
>
> Speak of Darius, the cursed one, who bore the mark of the Titan's wrath upon his very flesh and breath — he sits in the Temple of the Five and holds his face unbroken.
>
> Speak of the son of the god of battle, in whose veins runs the fury of the fighter himself, who rose from the margins of the world and shook it. *([[The Party/Xhazag (Kaz)|Xhazag]])*
>
> Speak of Athalea, who was born already mourning, whose voice carried the grief of a people and a city swallowed by the sea, who sought in the academy halls the name of what she had lost.
>
> Oh muse of memory long past, speak of Thylea. Speak of her time of crisis, in her hour of salvation. Speak, memory, and tell us the tale once more, in our time.

Then, somewhere very far from where the story would begin: a great boiling sea, and rising out of it a tower that pierced the heavens. At its top a great star had been imprisoned, casting a baleful red glow onto the turbulent waters below, while harpies screeched around the structure in the teeth of a fierce storm.

## Our Adventure

### Strangers in a Tavern

The fledgling heroes — Ellion, Darius, Xhazag, and Athalea — found one another in the warmth of a tavern, strangers bound by fate rather than choice. Each had been summoned by [[NPCs/Kyrah|Kyrah]], servant of the Oracle, and none of them knew the others. So they did the sensible thing and interrogated each other.

Ellion came first. He had only just met the others, but his reputation had arrived ahead of him: *a great hunter, and he talks to birds.* So had a less flattering rumor — that there was a weirdo around who painted his skin every single day and must be crazy. Ellion pointed out that plenty of people believe things because *they* are crazy, which does not make *him* crazy. The paint is common among his people. So are the birds: some of his people rely on them when leaving the cliffs, because from a bird's eye it is much easier to find things. When the talk wandered into how the world works, he laid it out simply — everyone and everything is part of nature, the lake and the waves and the forest and the trees, and somewhere along the way creatures forgot it.

Talk turned to art. It was proposed that once they had survived, won their glory and saved the world, they should perform the whole tale together — a play, or a song, or better yet both at once, with a lute behind it, set in a beautiful forest, with a forest spirit and a girl who fears for its trees.

### Ground Rules

Darius got the more pointed questions. *Were there snakes anywhere on his body? Was he a cursed bastard?* He said no. The concern, it was explained, was practical: cursed people are being hunted these days. Darius said he didn't subscribe to such notions. Others do, came the reply — and one of them would frankly prefer to travel with people who won't turn them into stone, won't get them hunted, and won't be wanting to kill them.

Then Kaz. *Did he, or did he not, kill babies?* He did not. He takes contracts on mortal lives, but not all of them: no babies, nothing involving sacrifices, no silencing. *"Be careful what you say right now,"* someone warned him mid-answer. It turned out to be a good answer.

And the horn. How had he lost it? Drinking. He had cut it off himself, after a night of drinking with a woman he did not know at the time was a goddess — that was only revealed to him afterward, and had he known, he would never have taken such a drink. He wears a metal one in its place for now, hopefully for good. It was, he maintained, a fair bet. What exactly the bet had been, he couldn't say. Someone suggested she'd wanted an epic poem about him; no, he already has many. An audience with her, perhaps? *"That might have been it. Then again, I don't remember. It was a lot of drinking."*

Darius, it emerged, will not be touched — and more than that, will not uncover his body to any living creature. This raised a cascade of practical questions from the others. How were they supposed to heal him? Pour a potion in? What if he needed CPR — which, they explained at length, involves the mouth and pinching the nose? How would anyone tell whether he was conscious, asleep, in shock, paralyzed, or dying, when any bleeding would be hidden under the armor? Did he sleep in it? He has a sleeping bag. The ground rules were settled: nobody removes his armor or clothing, whatever happens.

### The Task

Kyrah bore grave tidings. The Oracle had foreseen a creeping doom descending upon Thylea — an age of ruin stirring as the ancient Oath of Peace nears its end. The twin titans, long held at bay by that fragile covenant, grow restless once more.

Their first task: hunt down a monstrous boar that has been terrorizing the surrounding lands — no ordinary beast, but a creature of dark purpose unleashed upon the innocent by the twin titans themselves. Kyrah was sending groups out to find it, and the search area was the whole of the Highlands, because the thing does not stay in one place. The party needed somewhere to start. Two tables in the tavern looked promising, and Athalea was judged the best equipped to go and ask.

### The Hunters

At one round table sat three badly injured men, better armored than anyone else in the room, with supplies, traps and weapons that marked them as hunters. At the next table sat farmers, unhurt but in a foul mood.

*"Hunters, what's been bothering you today?"* Athalea asked. The answer was a huge beast — white. Not every white thing is cursed, but most cursed things are white. They had set baited traps in the middle of the forest for some other beast, heard a crashing in the bushes, and a huge white boar ran straight through their camp. It didn't even stop to do anything. That had been a few miles south of here, some days ago.

Athalea offered her help and set her terms: if their answers proved useful, she'd give them a discount. Between the three of them they had a broken arm, a broken leg and a bad flesh wound. She warned them she wasn't the best of healers, but thought she could at least help the flesh wound heal faster.

The hunters told her what they knew:

- It was ghostly white, with black, bloody eyes, and about the size of an ordinary highland boar — roughly half her height, maybe a bit more
- It was studded with spears, mostly hunters', and still moved very fast
- There was no blood on it at all, and a faint glow about it
- It seemed agitated by something, and it roamed frantically across the land
- It was drawn to fruit — rotten fruit especially
- They had only ever seen the one
- Most beasts in the area are mild unless you cross their territory, the griffins included; only a few have come near the farms

### The Back Room

Athalea asked whether anyone here performed rituals. No one did. So she went to see the injured man they'd laid on a cot in the back room near the kitchen, swaying slightly on the way. The wound was already infected, and whoever had stitched it had clearly never handled anything but red meat — nothing had been cleaned. She took out clean rags, cleaned it out as best she could, and whispered over it in a strange language. The man began to wake.

She came back out covered in blood, washed up in the kitchen, and gave the hunters a short lecture: before stitching anyone again, put the needle and everything else through alcohol first. The man would carry a scar, but he would be all right.

### The Farmers' Table

The farmers were not whispering, and everyone in the tavern heard their verdict: *these* are the heroes? Not much. They weren't exactly running to save anyone. They were drinking *kon* — barley, rose water and herbs.

Kaz bought a round of five, took two of the others with him, and walked up flagon in hand, which silenced the table instantly. He introduced himself, offered a show of goodwill, and explained that they were about to set out after the cursed boar and would welcome anything that might help.

It did not go well. The farmers were losing their homes, and wanted to know who had sent these people. One of them announced that this was the first time he'd talked to a cow.

*"I am not a cow. I am a minotaur. Cows do not have the capability to speak."*

The one in full armor, asked what *he* was, said he was just a man.

The farmers had never seen the boar and knew nothing of its habits or weak points. They knew what it had done: one had lost his whole house, and another his twenty acres. Kaz tried to reassure them — *"I know my size is intimidating, but I promise you I have only good wishes for you. That's why I bought you the drink"* — and was thanked with all their whole hearts: they would keep it on the shelf forever, as a gift from the great heroes. The whispering started again as soon as the party walked away.

Asked afterward how it felt to be treated like a monster, Kaz was philosophical. *"My size has often intimidated those less gifted. I try not to take it upon myself."* Sometimes it hurts. It comes from ignorance. He will be fine.

### Payment

Back at the hunters' table, one of them reached into his pocket and brought out a silvery trinket decorated with feathers, bearing the symbol of a goddess. He had found it over the summer and thought it had belonged to a healer. Athalea had only been able to help one of the three, and a healer at a temple would charge at least ten coins a head, so she accepted it as full payment. The hunters, grateful, arranged a hearty breakfast for the party before the hunt.

### The Map

The party regrouped to pool what they'd learned — the hunters' account, and the farmer whose twenty acres the boar had destroyed. Athalea took a blank piece of parchment from her bag and sketched it out: the tavern here, the hunters' camp a couple of miles off, and the search area to the south.
