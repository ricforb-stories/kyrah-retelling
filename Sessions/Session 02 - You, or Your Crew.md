# Session 2 — June 19, 2026 — "You, or Your Crew"

**Level:** 1
**Party Members in Attendance:** [[The Party/Ellion|Ellion]], [[The Party/Darius|Darius]], [[The Party/Xhazag (Kaz)|Xhazag (Kaz)]], [[The Party/Athalea|Athalea]]

## Our Adventure

### Morning at the Tavern

The party woke to a farewell breakfast of stew, bartered from the hunters they'd helped the night before and made, as the hunters put it, from the bottom of their simple hearts. Athalea had tried once more to teach them to clean wounds with alcohol; they still didn't see why anyone would pour good drink on a cut. Outside, [[NPCs/Kyrah|Kyrah]] sat facing away from the tavern with her scrolls and a feather, murmuring to herself, while [[The Party/Xhazag (Kaz)|Kaz]] did push-ups into the high hundreds — *"the strength of him was like he was ready to hold the world on his shoulders,"* as Kyrah narrated it.

Nobody in the party was much of a tracker. [[The Party/Ellion|Ellion]] offered to ask the birds.

### The Birds and the Trees

Ellion went to the nearest tree full of birds and addressed them in their own language, which to everyone else sounded like screaming. That got their attention. The trouble was describing what he wanted — none of them had ever seen the boar — so he conjured an illusion of it for them.

Every bird within at least a mile vanished from the sky, fleeing in terror of the creature. Most of them went the same way, toward the horizon.

The ground offered more. Around the tavern there were only a few sets of tracks, tangled up with the party's own: one pair, going by the hoofprints, heading off toward the city, and two others going in different directions — big feet in boots or sandals. One of those trails was pressed noticeably deeper than the rest, as if its makers had been carrying an injured man, and it ran in the same direction the birds had fled.

Ellion tried the trees next. He laid a hand on one, and it was plainly frightened, rustling with nowhere to run. Its roots didn't go nearly as deep as they should for a tree its size; there were claw marks on it, maybe from a feral cat; and it was more shaken than any tree they'd passed so far. It tried to tell him something and couldn't. *"They are not talking. Maybe they won't — but they cannot."* Between the birds fleeing his illusion and the trees refusing him, the others wanted to know what exactly he'd done to offend the shrubbery. He reminded them he was born from a tree.

Meanwhile [[The Party/Darius|Darius]], following the deep trail almost to the nearest hill, found a bloodied scrap of cloth about the size of a handkerchief, trampled into the track. He was asked, firmly, not to smell it. On closer inspection it was soft, fine leather — far better than anything the hunters could afford, when their clothes were no richer than a village peasant's.

From the top of the hill the party saw a shallow stretch of forest ahead, and beyond and to the right of it, over more hills, smoke. Too much smoke, and the wrong kind. [[The Party/Athalea|Athalea]] laid out the logic: if the boar had smashed through a farm's furnaces and hearths, flying sparks could burn a whole village down in hours — and the birds didn't want to go there either. They turned toward the smoke.

### The Burning Farm

An hour up and down the hills brought them over a rise to a ruined farm: acres upon acres of what had been crops, now ash, with the smoke still hanging over it. At the edge of it stood a family of about eight, staring at the wreck of their home — a man and a woman sobbing quietly, and children of ten to fifteen trying to calm them.

When Athalea asked whether they could help, the mother wailed louder. One of the boys answered. They had been gathering breakfast before heading to the fields, and the next moment there was fire everywhere, and rumbling from every direction, so loud — the barns torn to pieces, the fields torn up. *Maybe Pythor is mad at us.* They had nowhere else to go.

The farmhouse, about the size of the inn, was still burning inside: part of the roof and one wall had already collapsed. The farm's well had caved in on itself.

### The Well

Kaz took the well. It needed delicacy — one careless move and the whole shaft would collapse and be lost for good — so the largest person present, a seven-foot minotaur for whom most of the rocks were one-handed pebbles, set about removing them with his fingertips, one by one, like a wrestler threading a needle. Darius "helped" by standing behind him and holding his torso steady, as one spots a friend doing heavy squats, and singing. Kaz had asked him to point out the loose stones. He gave Darius a long, confused side-eye and thanked him for his assistance. Acrobatics 23; Athletics 20. Nothing fell into the well. The whole family, parents included, watched in silence.

With the well clear, Athalea lifted cube after cube of water with *shape water* and hurled it into the house (Arcana 18). Part of the roof came down, but the walls held and the fire went out.

### Guests of the House

Athalea suggested the family's grain store, underground near the northern wall, might have survived — enough to replant. But she wouldn't go poking around their property without an invitation. So one of the boys dropped to one knee, bowed his head, and formally invited them in as guests of the house. Then the whole family knelt.

They thanked Kaz as "Master Horny." He corrected them gently: his mother-given name is Kazak, and people only call him Horny by mistake, because he has horns. He tried to bow, remembered that last time he had headbutted a table, and did a small squat instead. The family confessed they would never manage to pronounce his name. *"For simplicity's sake, you can call me Kaz."*

Kaz knew the Thylean custom — a host must host, and a guest is expected to give a gift — and gave them one gold piece. To a farmer that is months of work. They took it almost shaking and bowed lower still. They could not rise, they said, because they had nothing to offer their guests, and that was a shame on their family. *"It is not. Your company, your friendship is all the gift we need."*

When they learned who the party were, they asked what they should tell people. Athalea, promoted on the spot to the group's public relations, said this could be where their journey began. The family declared they would build a temple on the spot to honor the heroes' first step. Athalea suggested that was a little early — perhaps in a couple of years.

### Ellion in the Field

Ellion, meanwhile, had wandered into the burned fields, and the land hit him. Everything around him was dying and screaming, an overwhelming suffering, and it knocked him to his knees — ten psychic damage — screaming in a language none of the others knew, hands over his ears. Athalea came close and hummed a lullaby to him. Kaz picked him up and carried him out to the hills, and the pain eased as he left the field; he was set down on the grass, and the grass withered around the shape of him with every breath he took. A little blood ran from his nose — clear sap that dried amber.

Asked whether the fire had started from any particular direction, he couldn't tell; it had burned out everywhere at once.

Later he went back to the edge of the field and made an offering to Thylea with seeds grown on this land: a small hole, the seeds, the earth patted flat, a prayer that she look after this land and the people, plants and animals who would live here. The ground felt as it always did, but the voice didn't come. The Mother did not seem to hear him today — or this land was too cursed to be held. The children asked whether, when his kind die, they turn into trees. He had never heard of it. He had never met another one like himself, he explained; perhaps they live in another forest. The family's faces grew sadder with every word, and eventually the father put a hand on his shoulder and told him he'd find his own one day.

### The Granary

The house was too unstable for Kaz to take even one step inside without bringing it down. So Athalea went in alone, light as a bird, while Kaz advised from outside. Her first attempt to cut through the floor, near a fallen beam, set the whole beam trembling violently. *"Slight suggestion,"* said Kaz. *"Ceasing your activities and reconsidering our position."* She tapped her way along the floor instead and found it sounding hollow at the far end of the room, and Kaz, with an architect's eye, pointed her to a spot between two planks near the column that wasn't burned and wasn't holding anything up. She cut there (Athletics 16, with advantage) and dropped straight through with the plank into the granary, landing on the sacks.

It was intact. The fire hadn't reached it. The grain was wet, for obvious reasons, but for a smaller farm it would be just enough to start over. And growing down from the ceiling, upside down, was a tree.

Kaz hauled the sacks out on a rope while Darius ferried them to the family, and the children followed Darius around with questions. *Was he made of metal?* No. *Why was he covered?* An oath, to his brothers and sisters and his goddess. *Wasn't it hot in there?* His goddess helped him. *How did he wash?*

### The Mountain Flower

One sack wasn't like the others. Darius opened it for Ellion, who recognized the seeds of a **mountain flower** — a tiny bluish flower with red dots, which strangely does not grow in the mountains. The father had never seen them before. The family grows rye, and there's no other way into the cellar but the northern wall.

Athalea, lowered back down by Kaz, cast *detect magic*. Only that one sack was magical, and the magic was **divination**. She sent the news to Ellion by *message*. They called Kyrah over to look, and she called back that she had found something of her own: a patch of unburned ground out in the fields.

It was about ten feet across, roughly circular, untouched by the fire, and covered in the same bluish flowers — the only living thing in all that ash. By the time anyone checked it for magic, there was nothing special about the flowers or the ground at all, and the seed they had carried over had lost whatever it held. Ellion dug one of the flowers out with a dagger, roots and earth and all, to take with him.

He also remembered a story, told as a play in a city square: a man lost his beloved and went after her deep underground, to the very roots of the world. On the way he disturbed the roots of the mother tree, and though he saved his love, he was turned forever into a small plant that grows beside the mother tree's roots. Ever since, it is said that whenever the Mother is angry with someone, these flowers grow, turning whatever is precious to them into flowers like these.

Ellion asked the family whether they'd had any unusual visitors. Only a group of travelers passing through toward the city, who had stayed two nights ago — two men and a woman, ordinary, honest people. They were the ones who'd told the family all about the party and the Oracle's vision: *the ground burning down, the gods fallen.* The family had thought it was a metaphor.

### The Tree

Then Kyrah, far off in the field, started screaming for them to come and see.

Kaz ran, with Athalea riding on his shoulder, one hand on his horn. In front of Kyrah stood a big tree split down the middle into two halves, and nailed into the split was a woman. Blood ran down around her. Snakes grew from the ground near the roots, among the bluish flowers. A bird was nailed to her chest, and in her hand was nailed a horn — one Kaz recognized. It looked a great deal like his own.

Athalea threw up. Kaz, who had seen bad things in his mercenary days, held (Constitution save 21) — but this was crueler than anything he'd seen before. Darius and Ellion came after; walking back through the dying fields cost Ellion more psychic damage, and when he arrived all that came up was sap, into a bag Athalea had emptied for him.

The message was clear enough. The bird, the horn, the snakes, the flowers: each of them. Someone knew what they were doing and how they thought, and had lured them here with the smoke. The family had likely lost their home just to set the scene. Whoever had done it was a step ahead of them.

Carved into the woman's face was writing that at first looked like a foreign language. It was a cipher, and it said:

*"Swear an oath here, or everyone you love will suffer the same."*

When the hair was lifted from her face, Darius recognized her. The last time he had seen that face she had been a little girl, ten years ago, before the ritual that would cover it forever — she had worn armor like his own, and she had been one of his order. *"She's my family."* She should never have been left unarmored, even in death.

Kaz asked whether it could have been a dragon. There were no tracks at all but their own. He admitted he had no family to threaten — they had vanished some five years ago, and he doesn't know where they are — and he didn't think anyone could do this to his father. But he worried about the rest of them. They were embarking on a great journey together to save the world; of course they were his friends. Dare he say family.

The horn troubled him. The goddess Kyrah should have his horn — how had it come to be here, if it was his?

### An Oath to Whom?

Before anyone touched the body, Kaz stopped them: this looked like a shrine, and disturbing a god's shrine was a bad idea. The party worked it through. It wasn't Lutheria's doing. The only god who demands blood sacrifice is Sydon — and the boar they had been sent to kill had been loosed on the land by Sydon and Lutheria, who would hardly be pleased to see them follow through. A follower of Sydon, then, trying to stop them. When they asked aloud whom the shrine had been made for, thunder rolled somewhere far away over the ocean.

At the end of the boar hunt they would have to choose in which god's honor to sacrifice and burn it; perhaps that was the oath being demanded, and swearing to burn the boar in Sydon's name would settle it. Nobody liked being bullied. Athalea, a devout follower of Lutheria who wants nothing to do with violence, didn't like it at all; Kaz was not keen on Sydon's messier demands, and wouldn't blindly swear an oath that could mean anything. They settled on leaving the shrine intact and burning the whole thing as it stood.

Kyrah, asked why strangers were going around telling everyone about the party and the prophecy, explained that the Oracle had prophesied to the whole land — the doom of Thylea, the end of time, every humanoid race killed — a month ago, and it was still going around. The Oracle had asked for heroes, and Kyrah had sent letters to everyone she'd seen in her visions.

Athalea asked the family for a large piece of cloth from the house, and they gave it freely.

### You, or Your Crew

Darius knelt, prayed, and gathered what dry grass and unburned wood he could find. He offered the sacrifice to Sydon, who had been angered — he said — by some unknown bastards interfering with his plans.

The party spent everything they had on it: both of Kaz's inspirations, Athalea's bardic inspiration, guidance. The total came to 14. Clouds gathered overhead and lightning formed in them, and a voice spoke in Darius's mind:

***"You, or your crew?"***

He answered. What he meant was that he would take it himself. What Sydon heard was his crew.

The lightning came down on everyone except Darius. They needed a 16 to survive it; the rolls were 10 and 11. Ellion took 31 damage against 15 hit points. As it fell, Kaz did the one thing he could — threw himself over whoever was beside him to shield them with his body. It was Kyrah, and he took her strike as well as his own.

When the dust settled Darius stood alone before the burning tree, his armor pristine and shiny. Ellion, Athalea and Kaz were dead. Kyrah crawled out from under Kaz's body.

Darius fell to his knees, beat the ground and screamed. It should have been him.

### Servitude

Kyrah had four resurrection scrolls with her, prepared for the boar fight, and they would only work for a minute after death. Darius begged her.

She wasn't sure. What was special about him? She wasn't looking for people — she was looking for heroes, special ones, who could change the fate of this world. *"What's special about me is that I'm still fighting, even now."* That wasn't enough. She needed to know they would not fail.

So he offered what he had, and she named it: **an oath of servitude.** At any point in his life, she could ask him anything, and he would have to do it. He swore it — Darius of his order, pledging his service to Kyrah.

Three scrolls were torn. Each of the dead had been taking the hand of a figure leading them somewhere peaceful and good, and was dragged painfully back out of it into a charred, ruined body, barely able to breathe.

### What It Cost

They came back badly burned, their wounds closing only very slowly, and marked by the resurrection itself: **-4 to all attacks, saving throws and ability checks**, fading by one with each long rest. Kaz, who had taken two strikes, came back with his legs terribly burned — his speed reduced by 5 feet, and a DC 10 Dexterity save after every Dash or he falls prone, until he receives magical healing such as *lesser restoration* or spends ten days doing nothing but resting. *"I can't feel my legs. Do I have my legs? Why does it smell like well-done steak?"* Everyone else carries lightning scars; Kaz has two forks of it across his back. Half their gear was charred — 31% of one pack, 48% of another.

And then Kyrah let something slip. Every group did something stupid. Not this exact thing — but something goes wrong every single time, and then she has to get the money, and the blood, and write the scrolls again, and send the letters again. How many times? **Eight.** She writes the scrolls in her own blood, because she can't afford the proper ink made from the mother tree; each takes about a month. She has one left. *"None of the groups died like that before. Spectacularly stupid."*

The resurrection also restores lost body parts. As they sat in the mud, Kaz's horn began to grow back, pushing the metal replacement out until it dropped to the ground. *"You're proper horny now." "I'm hornier than ever."*

### Rain

Heavy rain began to fall, sizzling on burned skin, and it hurt terribly. The party had opinions about Darius — Athalea wanted to know if she could eat his liver; Kaz planned to introduce him to Betsy, blunt side first — and a request: next time a god's voice starts asking vague questions in his head, ask a follow-up question. Darius insisted he had meant to sacrifice himself.

Then Darius took off his gloves — scandalous — to help Athalea pull out her burned feathers properly, gently, with bare fingers. His hands were very white and a little hairy; not all his hair, it turned out, is snakes. Asked what they'd seen while dead, Athalea said it was peaceful, far more than she'd expected. Ellion remembered nothing, like sleeping for a few minutes; his people simply reunite with nature. Someone asked what they'd make anyone do, if they could make them do anything. Re-establish the Oath of Peace forever, came the answer — something even the Dragonlords couldn't manage.

By two or three in the afternoon the rain stopped. Athalea gathered up a five-foot cube of rainwater and washed everyone off. Darius had a medical kit.

### The Burning

The tree was still standing, and the woman was still on it. Darius asked what he should do. The party's advice was simple: he could believe in anything he liked, including sheer terror of the god who had just fried his friends, but he should not call anyone a bastard in the middle of asking Sydon to listen.

In the end it was Athalea. She asked everyone to give her privacy — Kaz lay down in the mud and announced he wasn't there, then left Betsy with her for moral support — and she lit her half-charred pipe, closed her eyes, and sang in Primordial in a low shaman's voice. She invited the soul tethered to what lay before her into the greater realm; offered the earthly remains, which no longer mattered to the soul, to Sydon with respect for the one who had perished, so that the flesh would not go to waste and the spirit could roam free of earthly shackles. It sounded like it needed drums and dancers, and more people than just her; it sounded like how it should have been done.

In the middle of the song an ember from her pipe fell onto the tree, and it went up in flames. She kept singing. *May the soul of this person feast freely in the lands of the afterlife.* Ellion went to find the farmhouse basement to sleep in. The others watched the tree burn from a distance and listened to the song.
