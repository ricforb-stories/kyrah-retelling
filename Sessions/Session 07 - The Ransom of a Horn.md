# Session 7 — August 16, 2026 — "The Ransom of a Horn"

**Level:** 2
**Party Members in Attendance:** [[The Party/Ellion|Ellion]], [[The Party/Xhazag (Kaz)|Xhazag (Kaz)]], [[The Party/Athalea|Athalea]] (Darius's player was absent this session)

## Our Adventure

### Out of the Water

[[The Party/Darius|Darius]] was still inside the rock on the fruit island, and nobody but [[NPCs/Versi|Versi]] could get him out. Versi was a bubble's width away: held inside a great mass of animated water in the tunnel beyond the grotto, with two spear-armed fish-folk and a green-skinned woman around her, and [[NPCs/Kyrah|Kyrah]] collapsed in the doorway in front of them.

[[The Party/Athalea|Athalea]] dropped from the ceiling. The woman was surprised to see anyone else there. Athalea had never seen animated water before, but when things are inside other things, you pull them out — so she flew straight through it, caught Versi with her momentum and her talons, and came out the other side with her (11 and 16; a Dexterity save to slip the tentacles as the water tried to pull its prize back). Then she beat her wings for the tunnel mouth, ten feet up, Versi dangling beneath her.

From the moment she dropped, the others heard nothing. They only knew where she'd gone. Kyrah sat down where she was and began to sing. Ellion and Kaz dashed toward the tunnel, Kaz with two of Ellion's glowing green *magic stones* in his hand.

Before she dropped, Athalea had caught some of what the fish-folk were saying, bubbling away in Primordial: *we should move if we don't want to die here.* The woman had looked annoyed at that, but gone along with it. Now, as Athalea flew for the exit, she yelled down to Versi: *if you can do something about whatever this is, this is the time.* The woman lunged after her with enormous nails, trying to catch her feet — and missed, badly.

As soon as Athalea came out of the tunnel, her friends could hear her again.

### The Water, the Fish-Folk and the Hag

One of the fish-folk hurled a spear at Athalea (17 to hit, 7 piercing), then came at her again; she spent the inspiration she'd been *"saving for a rainy day, which is today,"* and the second strike went wide. They were trying hard not to hit Versi. They were very precise about it.

The water, meanwhile, was angry that it didn't have its thing anymore. It rolled out after the party, glowing a deep purple, and made it plain that anyone who tried to get past it toward Versi would be swallowed. Ellion greeted it — *"I am nature. Hello, my dear water"* — and tried to remember what the druids had ever said about water that walks. What came to him: *it is very weird water. Water should not be walking.*

Kaz, having spent a while in this grotto already, took a professional look at the ceiling. It wasn't built by anyone; the river had carved it over ages. Bringing it down would be like collapsing a mountain. So he did the next best thing: he grappled the nearest fish-man (19) and threw him into the boiling river — two instances of damage, he reasoned. On an Intelligence check (14), something else came to him as the fish-man went in. *They don't have a boat. How were they planning to get out?* The fish-man swam off through the scalding water with no trouble at all. *"They can swim in boiling water. We have problems."*

Then the woman came out of the tunnel and hissed something at the water, and they all got their first good look at her. Her skin bubbled and boiled, green and blue, and maggots crawled through it. She gave off an aura that disgusted more than it frightened, and made them want to stay as far from her as they could; mechanically, it frightened them. She looked around, piercing each of them with her gaze.

Kaz was the one it caught. He was on one hit point, half-blind with blood in his eyes, and what he could make out was mostly the smell — rot and maggots. He decided that death itself had come for him. *"First of all, I'm the son of a god, a great warrior. I'm not frightened. But you should really run away."*

She pointed a crooked finger at him and whispered: ***"Die."***

He failed the save (10), and dropped. On the ground, with the cold already gripping him, his heart gave one more beat, and another, and he stayed at one hit point on a Constitution save (17). The last thing he'd told them was *run away, and don't look back, no matter what happens.*

### "Do Something"

Versi was hysterical — crying, wailing, lost in sorrow — and Athalea wasn't sure she could hear anything at all. She tried anyway: if Versi didn't do something, all of them were going to die down here, and so would everyone still alive upstairs; they had saved some, and it would all be in vain. She rolled Persuasion (*"I am a bard. I was built for this"*) and kept climbing.

The fish-folk turned on Kaz. One saw his chest still moving and put a spear through his shoulder, where there was already one; another went into his ribs. Each time he held on (13, then twenty-something). His blood poured down into the river, and the water began to run red as well as boiling.

Then the water obeyed its mistress. It rose into a wave and crashed into Versi in Athalea's grip; blood spilled from Versi's mouth, and somewhere outside the grotto walls thunder boomed, as if something terrible had happened. The water swallowed her again and leapt into the river with her. Athalea grabbed after it, and her claws closed through nothing but water.

Ellion threw a magic stone into the hag's back for 12 and went looking for a rock to hide behind. There wasn't one.

The second fish-man rolled a natural 20 on Kaz and put the spear through his throat. Kaz spent his last inspiration and held on (20). He would not be speaking for a while.

Kaz, still terrified, turned around so he wouldn't have to look at her and swung blindly at nothing (11).

The hag went for Ellion and raked him with her claws for six. He stayed up.

Kyrah's song stopped for a moment. *"The water weird should be bound to some water! Try to take it from the hag!"* Then she went back to singing.

Athalea flew for a stalactite, kicked off it to build speed, and dove through the water weird a second time to pull Versi out (13). It worked, and the weird struck her as she passed through (19). She came out the far side, dropped, and fell to the ground unconscious. The last thing Kaz heard from her was a very faint *"Do something,"* aimed at Versi.

### The Horn

Kaz had been told he couldn't die, and apparently that was true — but his friends could, and by his count two of them just had. He didn't see a way out without divine intervention, and he couldn't ask for it if he was dead.

So he reached up to his horn, which had only recently grown back after many, many years, and broke it off: snap, crackle, pop.

He thought about whom to ask. His father first, but his father wasn't here. Sydon second, since they were surrounded by his images — but they were fighting his people. That left only one god he had ever actually met: the goddess Kyrah.

*"I dedicate all that I ever did, and all that I was supposed to do. I dedicate my future, my prophecy, everything unfulfilled, hand in hand with my own life. Save my friends, please."*

Then he pushed the horn into his own chest, all the way to the heart (8 damage). His enemies were visibly weirded out that he was finishing their job for them. He failed the first Constitution save (7) and kept breathing anyway. The fish-folk tried again and again; heart stabbed, shoulders stabbed, probably liver, and the only thing they hadn't tried was a head shot, which they had tried, but his skull was thick. He was in so much shock that he felt none of it. It was like watching a play.

The table granted him inspiration for it.

### The Cloud

Ellion had nothing left to cast. He wondered whether the hag was a fey creature like him (it didn't come to him), watched the water snatch Versi off the ground again and start hauling her away, and then asked for something that would take the water and the fish-folk but leave Kaz out. He gambled for it. Out of a point in the middle of the enemies burst a yellow, nauseating gas, twenty feet across, and the area went dark with it. He paid for the magic with three levels of exhaustion. He also blasted the hag with an *ice knife* (17 to hit, 13 damage).

Both fish-folk and the water failed their saves and spent their turns retching and reeling. Kaz rolled a natural 1 against the hag's fear, then attacked recklessly into the mist with his one good arm (the left one no longer moved), landing 15 bludgeoning on one of the fish-folk with a sound like hitting a wet tub.

Ellion hit the hag with another stone (9 to the head). Someone asked what she could be using to control the water — a bottle, perhaps — and nobody could see anything on her.

Kaz took his next swing (reckless, 20-something) and killed the wounded fish-man. By then he could barely lift his weapon, so he pulled the fish-man onto the javelin still sticking out of his own body and threw the corpse down in front of the other. The river between him and the rest of them was too wide to jump; it opened out toward the sea.

The hag turned to Ellion, frightened now himself, and pointed. *"Die."* He felt the cold wrap around him and pull at his soul, and he refused it. His soul was already promised. He had made a great sacrifice to his mother only a day ago, and he was not giving up that gift to *"some third-rate magician, some conjurer of cheap tricks. Nope. Nope. Nope."*

Athalea, meanwhile, came back from the edge on her own. She didn't stand up; she just started breathing again. She could turn her head and see what was going on, and barely make a sound.

### Stone

Then, out of nowhere, the hag had to make a Constitution save, and she failed it by more than five. Her figure began to become one with the cave, stone rising up through her from the floor. At that moment the water weird burst back into the river, and Versi, with a scream, fell into the boiling water. *Two problems solved and one created.*

The statue still radiated the hag's fear. Kaz couldn't go anywhere until someone broke it, and he couldn't get into the water either — *"If I get into the water, I die"* — so it was Athalea again (*"Why is it always me?"*). The prophet had about three rounds before she'd be a boiled prophet.

Ellion went at the statue with his stone and chipped off nine, halved by the stone. Kaz, still too frightened to look at it, threw a javelin at it from behind. It slipped out of his hand on the backswing and took Ellion in the head for six. The chipping stopped with one last big stone sound, and Ellion dropped (Constitution 8). *"This is karma."*

Athalea got Versi out of the water. Versi could crawl out, but had nothing left to stand with. She was scalded and boiled, and lay beside Athalea, two ladies looking at each other. The last fish-man, still retching in the cloud, ran and jumped into the river and was gone.

Kaz shook off the fear at last (17 on the Wisdom save) and went at the statue (18 to hit, 8 bludgeoning, four through the stone), shouting that there was still one alive in the smoke. *"What the hell do you want me to do with it? Go there? No way."*

Then he turned to Ellion, who had come round to death saves. Stabilizing was a DC 10 Medicine check. Kaz had nothing to bandage with, and Ellion's leather armor only covered his chest, so Kaz tore up his own trousers and left himself in shorts no man should be wearing (*"I'll buy new pants as soon as we get to a normal city"*). He tied a tourniquet above the wound and left the javelin in. Ellion's blood, like his vomit, smelled fresh and green. He would be out for three hours.

For that round, every one of them could reroll one die of their choice.

Athalea slung Versi on her back like a backpack (*"I'm doing smart things now"*) and flew her clear. Kaz hit the statue once more, and it crumbled; the pieces turned back to flesh as they fell. Behind him, the water weird rose out of the river again.

Kaz spotted two things in the remains (passive Perception 13): a shiny vial, and a torn piece of parchment. He picked up the vial, carried it to the shore, and held it up to Athalea. She lashed out with her feet, caught it in her talons, and crushed it (AC 13, two hit points). The water in it ran out. The weird didn't dissolve, but it stopped coming after them and began to circle in place.

Kaz picked up the parchment, then picked up Ellion. The parchment gave them the hag's name: **Helica**.

### After

Kyrah stopped singing and ran to them. Athalea sat on the shore with Versi's head in her lap. *"Well, you succeeded, huh? You're not dead."* It wasn't perfect, said Athalea — there was the carnivorous rock with Darius in it, and Ellion asleep — but Versi was here, and they were here, and she'd take a win when she saw one.

Kyrah suggested they rest in the quarters upstairs — *"Many of them became vacant today."* Athalea was worried about leaving Versi down here: what would stop them coming back? Darius could wait, said Kyrah; it wasn't as if he could cross to the other side without a proper burial. *"Aren't you supposed to be in the serious domain?"* Athalea hoped he wouldn't anger the ferryman and turn into a ghost in the meantime.

Then Kyrah turned on Kaz. Could they not do that anymore, stabbing themselves in the chest for the gods? The gods who want to help you will help you without sacrifices, she said, and the ones who only watch the blade will not intervene, not against Sydon. *"Sydon seems to care — in the way of killing us all."* *"It is more than killing you. He tried to take your destiny away from you. That's bigger."* Someone said he'd be furious with Helica for failing. Not that he could do much about it now.

The hag had been gruesome, Athalea said, and she'd enjoyed it. Versi was *"hella heavy"* for a pretty little thing. Kyrah thought she'd be all right: she had probably been boiled only because it had taken her by surprise, and that water shouldn't be able to hurt her.

Kyrah asked Kaz if he'd hurt his voice. He pointed at the gaping hole in his throat, a rag stuffed into it. There were four or five tortured bodies still to be carried upstairs for proper burial. Kyrah wanted to stay with Versi, and went to fetch the old father: he was the only one besides her allowed down here, and she thought he had one more prayer in him for the day, besides ointments and medicine.

At some point, Kyrah and Athalea talked about why Athalea had come. She wanted to find out what happened to her people. *"So you didn't kill them?"* *"How could I kill anybody?"* She hadn't been there. One day the village was simply empty: nobody gone missing, nothing burned, no mess. *"Well, you're in the perfect place if someone can give you an answer"* — the answer was lying in her lap.

They settled in for the long rest, Kaz insisting on the first watch against the water thing, the rock thing and the poison gas. Kaz told old soldier stories: the pilgrimage from his village up north down to the city, where he became a soldier. Kyrah fished out a very wet piece of paper and began, wobbly, to write.

### The White Tree

Ellion, unconscious, travelled mostly through darkness, until he came upon a single white tree. He stood at its roots. It was quiet and very, very old, its roots stretching for hundreds of Ellions in every direction, its branches rising further than he could see, and when he touched it he could feel its sap moving. It reminded him of the Mother Tree, as he had seen it: his Mother Tree. He tried to introduce himself.

A voice came back, a different voice with the same intonation he'd heard before from one particular creature: *"Fire. Fire."*

A rider swept into the vision on the back of a brass dragon, and the dragon burned the branches, and they turned to ash in front of him. Before he could move, it came on, burning, and as it came closer he could make out the rider on its back. It was him.

*"What the hell are you doing?"* he screamed up.

The rider glanced down, and answered in his own voice: *"This is our destiny, isn't it?"*

He woke to the face of an old cleric tending his wounds, who had brought his last healing magic down to bring him back. Ellion leapt to his feet and spent the first twenty seconds looking around for the dragon and its rider, not understanding that he'd stopped dreaming. All the damage that had put him down, it turned out, had been Kaz's javelin. *"I saw some unbelievable shit in the dream."* *"This whole thing is unbelievable, that we're somehow still alive."*

Kaz sat cross-legged with his eyes open, holding himself together, and Kyrah reminded him that she had told him he'd survive. Scars were nice. Battles, heroes. Versi was asleep, curled up with Kyrah on Kaz's hide. The rooms upstairs needed cleaning before anyone could sleep in them; four or five people had been sprayed across them. The old man said he'd see to the acolytes, and that they should rest. He sat down at Kaz's level, looked him in the eye, and told him that he was in the temple of Sydon, and it might bless his soul. Then he went on dressing Kaz's wounds. Ellion condensed water out of the air, stirred ointment into it, and gave it to Kaz to drink.

Darius was still under the rug, waiting for Versi to wake up and call it off.

### Why Hire a Hag

In the quiet, the old man explained what they'd walked into. They were all starting the quest. If Versi was never there when they arrived, they wouldn't know what to do, where to go, or how to reach their destinies, and they would never rise to be heroes. A great many destinies were tied to that, and the party was one of the last he could think of. If no one can receive a prophecy, no one knows what to do.

He said that nobody in Sydon's own temple would have done it. No one would go against the temple, and serving Sydon did not mean performing his will blindly. So whoever wanted this had to hire people from outside, people not devoted to Sydon. And a god cannot simply intervene and pronounce his will like that; it isn't how things are done. If a god did, the five-hundred-year-old oath would be broken, and he would not be on the good side of it. Besides, she is an oracle, and a daughter.
