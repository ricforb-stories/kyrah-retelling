# Session 12 — October 16, 2026 — "She Is Listening"

**Level:** 2
**Party Members in Attendance:** [[The Party/Ellion|Ellion]], [[The Party/Darius|Darius]], [[The Party/Xhazag (Kaz)|Xhazag (Kaz)]], [[The Party/Athalea|Athalea]]

## Our Adventure

### Five Minutes to Sundown

The sun was low and red over the steppe. From the north wall they could hear hooves, a lot of them, walking toward the city. They had about five minutes.

The plan needed a resurrection scroll, and [[NPCs/Kyrah|Kyrah]] had none. She could write one fast with enough of a god's blood, which meant [[NPCs/Pythor|Pythor]]'s, which meant [[The Party/Xhazag (Kaz)|Kaz]] running up the hill to the palace. He had already started running when he had to decide, with no time to talk it over: the hill, or the other blood. The party still had the bird-skull bottle, the magical blood that smelled of copper and ash. He turned back and handed it over.

Then a second thought caught him (Wisdom 8, and with inspiration, 18): if the city fell tonight, nobody would ever find [[NPCs/Anora|Anora]] in the dungeons. *"Do you need me for your plan to work?"* He wasn't performing. *"If you want to go, go."* So he ran: through the festival, up the hill, down the corridors to his father's statue, without stopping once (Athletics 24). He made it in about a minute.

### The Script

Kyrah took scroll and ink from her case. There was nothing near the wall to write on, so she and [[The Party/Athalea|Athalea]] sprawled on the cobblestones. At some point there was muttering and arguing, and then one of Athalea's feathers was plucked out. *"It's for the cause."* *"It's painful."* *"It will be much more painful."*

They wrote from the end backward, page by loose page, and kept dropping them in a pile for the performers: the Song first, then the Litany of the Lady's names, the Vigil, the Naming, the Cup, and the Proof. Athalea was stressed and very proud, and rolled everything at a disadvantage, and still kept landing 18s. The one page they couldn't write was the story of how Darius had met the Lady when he was dead. That he would have to tell himself.

Partway through, Athalea put down her page and started undressing, as if it were a normal Tuesday. She was mostly feathers anyway. By the end she was smeared in a mixture of wine, salt and charcoal. Her plan for the performance, she told Darius, was to be all over him, and he was to behave as if it were the most normal thing in the world.

### "They Made Me Make the Deal"

[[The Party/Ellion|Ellion]] had something to share in that minute. The centaurs knew there was a cursed person in the city. *"There are a lot of cursed persons."* No: like *him*. And they probably wanted to see him.

[[The Party/Darius|Darius]] was shaken by it, and stayed shaken for the rest of the night. Nobody had to wonder how the centaurs knew; only one of them had talked to the centaurs. *"It's not like I made the deal,"* said Ellion. *"They made me make the deal."* Someone suggested that an Oath of Fellowship, back in the clearing, would have covered not spilling each other's secrets.

### Dressing the Champion

A tin can with some scarves round it would not do, and there was a whole costume room by the amphitheater. Darius came back from it unrecognizable: a desert veil-turban wrapped over his head and face, black and green, with a great deal of fancy on top. Loose trousers, and a thick sash hung with ribbons. Too much, in a deliberate way. He looked as if he belonged on the steppe.

Then he read the script, every page he was given (Religion 21). For a moment he felt like a real champion of the Lady. The instructions were perfectly clear, and there were almost no words in them. His friends, he concluded, knew him well, which he appreciated, and which was also quite offensive.

The rule on every page: nobody says her name. Not once. Titles only: the Lady, the Black-Eyed Lady, the Mistress of Death, the Lady of Dreams. She might actually hear.

### Anora, Again

Anora was on her gilded bed and didn't look up. *"If you have not come to let me out, Father, I am in no mood to speak."* Then she saw who it was, recovered her composure, and stood. *"Nice to see you, Camille. Why are you not at the theater?"*

Kaz explained: one of his companions might have told the centaurs about a chosen one, and now they were coming at sundown, which was in a few seconds. *"That makes no sense."* *"It doesn't. I have the idea Ellion might have left a few things out."* His friends had a plan. But if the city fell, nobody would ever find her down here. *"That is in no way dreadful at all."*

And there was the oath. *What exactly did you tell Father? Word for word.* Kaz had it written down: *"when I go down now to talk to my sister in the dungeons, I will not attempt to free her."* He'd meant *now* to cover that one visit. That depended, said Anora, on how their father had taken it.

She ate bread while he thought. She told him that their father's logic would be simple: the dungeon is the bunker under the city, the safest place there is. Where were the other entrances she'd mentioned? *"You won't find them. It's a labyrinth."* *"I know my way around those."* She would draw him a rough sketch. *"Don't worry about me. I'm most probably dead in two weeks."* He went to talk to their father.

### Like Father, Like Son

Pythor was in the throne room with a spear in his hand, shouting into the face of his own statue about failure and emptiness, and how everything he had would soon fail.

*"Father, I need you to sober up. Otherwise, very soon, everything you know will be gone."* *"I know. Come here, boy."* There was an emergency. *"There is always an emergency."* The centaurs were attacking at sundown, and outside the window it was sundown. Why? Kaz wasn't sure; it had something to do with Ellion. He asked to be released from his oath so he could get Anora out.

Pythor would not: the dungeon was the safest place in the city. *"So you're just going to sit here drunk and not do anything?"* Hadn't he mentioned? That had been the deal for the last five hundred years.

And he couldn't have released him anyway. *"You do not make an oath to me, son. You make it to Thylea herself. I can say whatever I want to say. The oath will stand."* What had *now* meant, then? *"What do you think, Camille? I was playing with oaths before you'd filled your first diaper. You think I would have accepted an oath that let you go down, come out, and go back to free her?"* That had been exactly Kaz's thinking. Then how had he taken *now*? *"As long as you live."* What happens to oathbreakers? Many things: a curse, in whatever form the magic of Thylea chooses.

*"You're just like me. You mess everything up. Like father, like son."* Kaz asked for fatherly advice, from the father who was never there. *"I have one really good one for you. Never make an oath to a god."* A bit late. The choice was Kaz's: risk being an oathbreaker, or go back to his friends and try to fix things.

*"If I don't see you again, Father…"* This time Pythor called him son, not Camille. *"I love you."* *"I love you too, son. And even if I'm gone, I will still be protecting you and your sister. You might not agree with my methods, but I will."*

### Anora's Answer

Back in the cell, Anora was on her second piece of bread. *"I assume you're free of your oath?"* He wasn't. If he freed her, he'd be an oathbreaker; he might get turned into a toad, and then he couldn't protect anyone. *"Up until now I didn't see the resemblance between you and Father. Now I do."* *"I've seen it from the moment I met you."* *"Is it the beard?"*

She handed him the map, as promised. No god in existence could have read it. Kaz, a minotaur, has a gift for labyrinths, but for walking them, not for reading maps of them. Where did the exits lead? She had no idea.

So he fell back on the only thing that had helped him all campaign: architecture (Intelligence, with advantage). Should the hidden ways be sealed, in case the centaurs knew them? Things came to mind. Estoria was named after a general, **Estor**, one of the very few humans who ever swore an oath to the Lady and had it accepted, and the rumors said that was why certain people could reach the city who should not have. If the fey still kept that knowledge, they might share the passages among themselves. But if they knew the passages, why hadn't they used them? And if the city fell, those passages would be Anora's only way out. Or someone could seal them from outside and leave her to starve in the dark, with no one coming: a slower death, and a better sacrifice, than the basilisks. He decided against sealing them.

That left the question of whether he trusted his friends with his sister's life. He decided to stay, at least to buy her time if anyone came. Anora would not have it. If he died down here defending her, he would never fulfil the Oracle's prophecy, and then they were all doomed anyway. *"I heard rumors you were beaten by a girl at the sparring grounds."* *"How did you hear rumors in the dungeon?"* *"I was not born in the dungeon."* Yes, but she was hella strong. And he'd been supposed to go on a date with that girl today. *"That's ironic."*

*"Join your friends. You're supposed to be with them, not with me. Don't risk being an oathbreaker. Don't risk dying here for me. I finally got to meet you. So go and make sure that we meet again."*

Anyone coming for her would still have to get through the bars, which would give him a few minutes. He went back to his friends.

### Rehearsal

Ellion learned his lines (a 23, after Athalea coached the pair of them on delivery). He'd be speaking Sylvan. The Common parts were transcribed for Darius so he'd know his cues by their sound. Athalea's rule for interruptions: one phrase for all of them. *Hush. She walks.* Ellion tied three bottles of wine to his belt.

Then they rehearsed Darius dying. He had to be happy about it. Athalea took a metal plate off his armor and swung it at him like a golf club (it was heavier than she was). *"Be happy! Forget your troubles!"* (Constitution 21.) Not a single sound came out of him. She gave him an inspiration for it.

Kaz came back. *"Did you bring another type of blood?"* *"Just the one in my veins."* *"Told you so,"* said Kyrah.

She held up the bird-skull bottle. It wasn't Pythor's blood, and it wasn't three liters. It may not work. *"Everything's going to be all right."* They could still abandon the city and run, someone pointed out. Nobody took that option.

It would take about ten to thirty seconds after he died, Kyrah said, and then he should rise. If it worked, it would hurt even more than dying and coming back the last time: he'd be exhausted, with no strength left to smile, and they would still have the oath to get through, so he had to act as if it were nothing. And one more thing. *"If, while you're dead, you see her — whose name we're not saying — don't do anything stupid."*

The cursed one would wait. Only after the proof, they agreed, would Darius show the centaurs that the champion and the cursed one were the same man. Before that, they couldn't touch him. Nobody touches a preacher.

Kyrah would give one signal from behind the wall: a single, very wrong note on her lute, to say the scroll was ready. If the others didn't hear it over the music, Athalea would.

Then the cry went up outside the walls. Horns sounded from three directions. The sun was down.

### The Gate

Athalea dropped everything she had beside Kyrah. Kaz did the honors with the gate. The north gate swung open onto the bridge, and the steppe beyond it was full of centaurs with arrows and javelins raised. At their front stood the [[NPCs/Hylonome|centaur woman]] Ellion had met, her weapon on her back. Nobody spoke. Nobody attacked yet. They were waiting to see who came out.

Ellion walked out alone, slowly, and stopped ten paces from her. Bowstrings creaked.

*"Child of Thylea, you are back. The sun is down. Have you brought the two you promised us?"*
*"Hear me. The Lady has sent a champion."*
*"And where is he?"*
*"He comes now."*

He stepped aside, and Darius came out: slowly, light and supple on his feet, shield and spear in hand. He planted the spear and said nothing, and stood with his head a little bowed, patient as a stone.

### The Litany

Then came the rustle of feathers. Athalea came down the wind in a long, smooth glide, and began the titles in a mix of Sylvan, Common and Celestial that was very hard to follow. *Lady of Dreams. Mistress of Death. Goddess of the underworld. She bids us dream. She bids us dance. She bids us feast. She gave the heart its joy again. She stands at death's door. She walks in dreams.*

The herd was supposed to answer. Ellion was supposed to get them answering, and didn't, and heard about it from Athalea afterwards.

It landed anyway (24, 12, and an 18 with one of Athalea's inspirations). The whole thing came off, however confusing it was, and Ellion's part was so monotonous and preacher-like that the centaurs mostly watched the strange apparel and listened to the rise and fall of the voices. So they did the Vigil to give them time to listen in.

### The Vigil

Darius set his spear in front of him, pointed at the centaur woman, and went down on his knees. Ellion spoke: night is the Lady's hour. When the captain Estor swore his oath to her, it was sworn in the dark, and not sealed until first light. Nothing is decided while she walks. Athalea flew right up to the woman's face, then back, then landed and walked in slow circles around the kneeling champion. *"You will not speak. You will not move. Do not speak over her."*

Hundreds of eyes burned into Darius's back. After a minute and a half the front line began taking tiny steps toward him, out of curiosity or something else, toward the movement under his veil. Athalea let a feather drop onto Ellion's shoulder. *"Hush. She walks."* Ellion touched Darius on the shoulder; Darius raised his head, took his spear and stood (14 and 14, and 18 with Athalea's inspiration).

*"Who walks, child of Thylea?"*

She asked, so they told her. Athalea went through the names again, flying to the centaur woman, to Darius, to Ellion, twirling in the air between them like a dance. They were paying a lot more attention now, for good or bad reasons.

### The Naming

Ellion (a 20; Darius a 17): *"She greets every soul at her door by name. In two days, one of you meets her champion. She would know whom to expect. Come forward one by one and be named to her."*

*"Are you preaching her name to us, child of Thylea?"* Athalea, overhead: please hold your questions until the end of the presentation.

The centaur woman stepped up to Darius. Athalea was draped over his back like a cloak, her head on his shoulder, whispering. *"What name do you give me, champion?"*

He froze. The script had given him no words here, and for a long moment he wasn't sure whether he was meant to name her, or name a champion, or what. Then: *"The Lady asks your name. Your mother's name. Your herd."*

She gave her name, Hylonome, and stepped back, and a murmur went through the herd. She had been testing whether he knew the ritual. Another centaur stepped forward, and then another. Each one gave their name and their mother's name and their herd, twice, and Darius tied a ribbon to his spear, and after each name, softly: *She is listening.* Having called on the naming, they couldn't refuse the Lady. Somewhere behind, a voice in the herd muttered that she has no champions, and used her name to say it. A chill went through the party. There was a chance she could hear now.

Athalea counted them as they came, and sent the count to the party by *message* from high above: **1,041 centaurs**. No children. All armed, though not soldiers as the city would understand it. About as many as everyone living in Estoria, soldiers included. Kaz took the number to [[NPCs/Stivon|Stivon]] on the wall.

And Ellion, glancing out across the steppe (Perception 17), saw some of the far hills moving. They were drawing closer: cyclopes, coming toward the city.

### The Grey Plain

The page nobody could write came next. Darius asked for a moment, walked over to the wall out of earshot, and came back.

Behind the wall, Kyrah was writing the scroll, dipping into the bottle. With every line she got paler. She turned aside, coughed blood, and kept writing; her quill was barely moving. Athalea had found a little drum somewhere on the walls and sat beating it, low and shamanic.

Darius faced the centaur woman, with a slight smile. *"You want to know how I was chosen? By the Lady of Dreams."* Yes, she said; they would very much like to know how this most unlikely event had come about. Please, entertain them.

He had been at the river, he said, about to step onto a boat, when he heard her voice. She told him to go and fight. *"Hush."* *"Don't hush me,"* said someone in the crowd: who was telling him about fighting? Darius turned his head toward them. *"Two days. She said my way is waiting for me, and I woke up."* Who was waiting? *"My destiny. My doom. Everything is waiting for me."*

*"If she told you only this,"* said the centaur woman, *"are you not supposed to be on our side? Or is the Mother's will against us now?"*

The drumming got louder. Athalea's voice came into his head by *message*: death is eternal; the Lady has all the time in the world; nothing needs rushing. Then, to the herd: *"You are trying to hurry fate. The signs were written. The signs were read. Two days. The Lady has spoken. Her brother-husband has spoken. Do you want to speak against their word?"*

*"And how did she tell you that you are to stand on Estoria's side?"* *"I was sleeping, and suddenly I heard her voice. I remembered her voice from the time before."*

He did. Once you have heard it, you cannot mistake it for anything else. She seeps into every bone. She slips into every drop of blood that leaves your body as you die. She feels like a last embrace, and a last goodbye on your cheek. No drug and no wine can dilute her presence.

It held (Athalea's Performance 21). Behind the wall, Kyrah plucked her lute: one very sour note. Few heard it over the drums. The scroll was ready. The crowd still looked mildly entertained.

### The Cup

Athalea dropped the drum with a thud. Ellion: *"She commands her children to drink, to dance, to feast, for any day may be the last. Her champion goes to no contest unfeasted. Listen: the city keeps her wine festival tonight. Drink with him."*

He opened a bottle and drank, very, very slowly. Athalea zipped about in the dusk and stole a drop. Ellion opened another bottle and said it again. *Drink with him.*

Kaz came out through the gate with a barrel of wine. His arms were full, so he'd opened the door with his leg. He set it down and went back for another.

Athalea sent Kyrah a *message*: drink some water, lie down, don't pass out. *"I'll do my best."*

*"I do like your approach, child of Thylea,"* said the centaur woman. *"The one cursed by our father serves you."* She meant Kaz. Kaz kept a face like a blank sheet, as Athalea's voice in his head told him to: *whatever you do, don't say a word. Don't react.* When he came back with the second barrel: *"Come, cursed human."* Athalea, in his head: *don't talk to them. Don't look at them. Do your job. Ellion will handle the rest.* He put the barrel down and went back for more.

*"Did he just defy me?"* *"He is my servant,"* said Ellion. *"He obeys only me."* *"So you do not wish to indulge your guests."* *"The champion welcomes you to drink with him. And the drink must be delivered."* *"Very well. We shall wait."*

*"Did you bring enough chalices for us? Or can your pet donate?"* A great chalice could be made from a horn, and it had to be the horn of the cursed human. Kaz had a spare, and that one unscrewed. They didn't want the spare. They wanted the horn attached to his head, as a piece of him to humiliate him with.

Ellion began to cut. At the first drop of blood from the horn, a bolt of lightning came out of the dry sky and struck the ground right in front of the centaurs. Athalea swooped low over them. *"Take what is not yours. Take the sacrifice that never belonged. See what happens."* The centaur woman looked down at the scorched ground in front of her hooves. Nobody mentioned the horn again.

The festival barrels had cups on strings, and Kaz hadn't taken the time to remove them. They needed more. He went up the wall to the soldiers. *"Cups might just save us. You don't need to know more. Just get me cups. As many as you can find."* Ellion poured from cup to cup, slowly, with Kaz silently helping his master (Performance 23, Kaz rolling for Ellion). Darius was still drinking the same cup of wine he had started half an hour ago, and somehow there seemed to be more in it than when he started.

### The Song

*"When the drinks are done, the child of the Lady will sing to her mother. You will answer her."* Athalea sang to the drums from the city, and the herd answered: *She is listening.*

> *They did not ask how long they'd stay. They drank as if the feast had just begun.*
> *She waited. She can wait. Death is patient. So is fate.*
> *Their scales were bronze, their breath was flame…*
> *She walks in dreams. She came to us in sleep.*
> *She showed us what the darkness eats, and what the dead ones keep.*
> *When the last breath leaves your chest, someone comes and takes your hand*
> *and leads you somewhere soft and good, softer than you'd understand.*
> *It is not pain. It is a feast you cannot see,*
> *and every cup is filled again, and every seat is kept.*
> *Tonight, tomorrow. Maybe you.*
> *She keeps a place for every guest.*
> *She keeps the champion's place the best.*

At the last line she walked behind Darius, wrapped herself round him like a cloak, and whispered: *she is listening.*

### The Proof

As he began to spread his arms, she kissed him on the cheek and flew up.

Darius opened his arms wide and looked the centaur woman straight in the eye, smiling. *"You asked for proof."* *"Indeed I did."* *"The Lady proves herself. I do not."* He stared, and smiled, and waited. It was very uncomfortable, and she could not look away. *"Choose your best spear. I will not raise mine."* He let his own spear fall.

She became the giant scorpion, and her tail went straight into his chest. Then a second time. He stayed on his feet to the very last moment, and died standing, and then he fell.

She turned back, and looked at the body. *"I assume that's it for the champion of the Great Mother."*

Nobody went for the body. The herd was laughing. High above them, Athalea was laughing too, wildly, wheeling round and round, and from up there she could see that the scroll was no longer in Kyrah's hands. She had finished reading it.

Last time it had taken about ten seconds. Now it had been a few minutes. The herd went on laughing, and turned to look at the child of Thylea.
